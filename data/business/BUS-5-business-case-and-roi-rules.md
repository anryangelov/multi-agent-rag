# Business Case and ROI Rules for Automation

Every request for a new automated process includes a short business case.
This guide explains how to write it so that requests can be compared.

## What to count

1. **Hours saved per month:** cases per month multiplied by the minutes
   saved per case, divided by 60.
2. **Errors avoided:** the number of rework cases per month today and the
   expected number after automation.
3. **Build effort:** analyst and developer days, estimated by the process
   analyst.
4. **Running cost:** connector licences, RPA robot capacity and calls to
   the cloud language model.

## How to value it

- One hour of employee time is valued at the standard internal rate
  published by Finance each January.
- Payback period = build cost divided by monthly net saving.
- A process with a payback period longer than **12 months** needs a
  written reason, such as a regulatory requirement or a risk that the
  automation removes.

## Benefits that are not hours

Some processes are worth automating even when they save little time: they
remove a compliance risk, shorten a customer waiting time or make a process
auditable. Describe these benefits in one or two sentences each; do not
convert them into fake hours.

## Tracking

Three months after go-live, the process owner and the analyst compare the
real numbers with the business case (see the Process Analyst Handbook,
BUS-4). If the real saving is less than half the estimate, the process is
reviewed at the next portfolio review.
